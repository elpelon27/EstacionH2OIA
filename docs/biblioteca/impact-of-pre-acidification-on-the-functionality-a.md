## Hechos clave (Qwen local)

- **Fact:** Low-moisture part-skim mozzarella (LMPS mozzarella) is the most consumed cheese in the United States with a per capita consumption of 12.29 pounds annually.
- **Fact:** The use of low-concentration-factor ultrafiltered (LCF-UF) milk for cheesemaking has become popular, especially for increasing the casein (CN) content of milk.
- **Fact:** LMPS mozzarella often suffers from impaired functionality due to lower moisture and firmer texture compared to regular mozzarella.
- **Fact:** Pre-acidification (PA), a technique involving adding an organic acid like acetic, citric, or lactic acid to cheese milk before the addition of starter culture, is used to improve the body and functionality of cheeses.
- **Fact:** The use of PA in LMPS mozzarella made from concentrated milk has not been extensively studied until now.
- **Fact:** Citric acid was found to be effective in reducing insoluble calcium levels during the cheese manufacturing process compared to other acids like acetic or carbonic.
- **Fact:** Cheese manufactured with a pH value of 6.40 and citric acid showed better composition, textural, functional, and sensory properties at various storage times (14 days).
- **Fact:** The study found that different pH values and types of acids used for PA in concentrated cheese milk (~4.2% CN) had varying effects on the calcium losses during cheesemaking.
- **Fact:** Microstructural properties of LMPS mozzarella were also investigated, including unmelted and melted states.
- **Fact:** The study aimed to establish different pH values and acid types used for PA in concentrated cheese milk (~4.2% CN) to understand their effects on the composition, textural, functional, and sensory properties of LMPS mozzarella.
