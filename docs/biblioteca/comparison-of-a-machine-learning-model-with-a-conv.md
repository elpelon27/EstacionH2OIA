## Hechos clave (Qwen local)

- **Fact**: Machine learning (ML) models were trained to identify intramammary infections (IMI) in late-lactation cows at dry-off, compared to a rule-based algorithm used on dairy farms.
- **Fact**: The study analyzed data from 3,645 cows approaching dry-off from 68 US dairy herds.
- **Fact**: Outcome variables of interest included IMI caused by all pathogens, major pathogens, and SSLO (Streptococcus and Streptococcus-like organisms).
- **Fact**: Features used for ML models were derived from cow-level descriptive data, clinical mastitis events records, milk volume, SCC (Somatic Cell Count), fat, and protein concentrations.
- **Fact**: Machine learning algorithms evaluated included logistic regression, decision tree, random forest, light gradient-boosting machine, naive Bayes, and neural networks.
- **Fact**: A conventional rule-based algorithm classified cows as high risk for IMI if they had one or more high SCC (>200,000 cells/mL) tests or ≥2 cases of clinical mastitis during the lactation period.
- **Fact**: Area under the curve (AUC) and Youden's index were used to compare ML models with the rule-based algorithm for all IMI outcomes.
- **Fact**: ML models had slightly higher AUC and Youden’s index values than the rule-based algorithm, but these improvements in prediction accuracy were not considered sufficient for replacing the current method.
- **Fact**: The study recommends that producers use a conventional rule-based method for implementing algorithm-guided selective dry cow therapy (SDCT).
- **Fact**: Rapid culture systems, data comparison algorithms, and the California Mastitis Test may be appropriate methods for identifying infected quarters in SDCT programs.
- **Fact**: Algorithm-guided protocols use routinely collected farm data to assess risk at dry-off and implement targeted dry cow antibiotic therapy.
- **Fact**: Previous studies have shown that switching from blanket dry cow therapy (BDCT) to selective dry cow therapy (SDCT) can reduce antimicrobial usage by up to 50% while maintaining similar health benefits.
- **Fact**: Recent clinical trials indicate that SDCT protocols pose negligible health risks if accurately identifying infected quarters and cows is included in the protocol.
- **Fact**: Machine learning models have been used for predicting late-lactation IMI, with SCC (Somatic Cell Count) identified as the most valuable predictor of IMI status.
- **Fact**: The study focused on logistic regression but did not explore non-regression ML approaches such as tree-based or neural network algorithms.
- **Fact**: The model-building process employed a stepwise evaluation of pre-specified combinations of predictors and their interactions, contrasting with common ML workflows where algorithms autonomously determine the most important feature combination.
- **Fact**: There is interest in developing machine learning algorithms that can detect IMI more accurately to limit antibiotic use to cows with actual infections (maximizing diagnostic specificity) and minimize udder health risks from failing to identify and treat infected cows (maximizing diagnostic sensitivity).
- **Fact**: The study aimed to develop ML models for identifying IMI in late-lactation cows and compare their performance to a rule-based algorithm currently used on dairy farms.
- **Fact**: Interest exists in developing algorithms with less diagnostic error so that antibiotic use can be limited to cows with actual infections (maximizing diagnostic specificity) and minimizing udder health risks from failing to identify and treat infected cows (maximizing diagnostic sensitivity).
