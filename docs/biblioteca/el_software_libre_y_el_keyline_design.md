## Hechos clave (Qwen local)

- The characterization of soil properties revealed significant differences within small spatial scales (less than one hectare). This highlights the importance of local variability in soil characteristics.
- High variability in soils can significantly affect expected outcomes, emphasizing the need for multiple tests when making decisions about cultivation.
- The establishment of soil management systems should be based on soil property characterization to minimize negative effects, promote conservation, and mitigate degradation impacts from climate change.
- The keyline method considers factors like scale of permanence, hydrological design, terrain morphology, physical-biological properties, and environmental aspects to establish a soil management pattern that benefits both crops and the environment positively.
- Various soil management systems had significant impacts on soil hydrogeological properties.
- The keyline system showed the highest porosity total (47.1795%), followed by the testigo method (43.0372%), cincel (42.078), and rastra (40.3168%).
- The keyline system also demonstrated the highest stability of soil aggregates, indicating its effectiveness in maintaining soil structure over time.
- **Canavalia Growth:** The canavalia growth was significantly influenced by different soil management systems, with the testigo method showing the best results.
- Continue using the keyline method due to its comprehensive approach.
- Conduct further studies on the impact of different soil management systems on various crops, including canavalia.
- Implement conservation practices based on soil property characterization and hydrogeological design principles.
