## Hechos clave (Qwen local)

- Deliberate delay of ovulation induction and timed artificial insemination (TAI) was effective in promoting the expression of estrus before AI.
- The study involved 4,672 lactating Holstein cows from two commercial dairy farms using sensors for automated detection of estrus.
- Cows were synchronized with a Double-Ovsynch protocol up to the first PGF2α (PGF-L) of Breeding-Ovsynch portion.
- Treatment groups G56 and G80 had induction of ovulation delayed by 56 hours and 80 hours, respectively, from PGF-L to GnRH2 before AI.
- Cows in the G80 treatment received AIE (automated artificial insemination) more frequently than those in G56 and had higher P/AI (pregnancy per AI).
- Delayed induction of ovulation led to increased ovarian function, as evidenced by larger follicles before ovulation.
- Cows with estrus had greater circulating concentrations of progesterone after AI compared to cows without estrus.
- The study found no significant difference in overall P/AI between the G80 and G56 treatments.
- Delayed induction of ovulation improved first-service reproductive outcomes, including higher P/AI for heifers (Knickmeyer et al., 2019; Kasimanickam et al., 2020) and cows in some experiments (Bishop et al., 2017).
- The findings suggest that delaying induction of ovulation can enhance first-service reproductive performance, particularly for heifers.
- The study supports the use of "split-time protocols" where non-estrous females receive GnRH and TAI a day later than those detected in estrus.
- Delayed induction of ovulation may lead to different ovarian function outcomes compared to immediate induction, affecting first-service reproductive performance.
- Cows with estrus had larger follicles before ovulation, higher ovulation risk after AI, and greater circulating concentrations of progesterone post-AI.
- The study recommends the use of automated detection systems for estrus to identify cows that are more likely to express estrus before AI.
- Further research is needed to explore the effects of delayed induction of ovulation on fertility during synchronization protocols for timed artificial insemination (TAI).
- Delays in induction of ovulation and TAI can be beneficial, especially when combined with other strategies such as estradiol supplementation or extended proestrus periods.
- The findings suggest that delaying induction of ovulation could potentially increase overall P/AI by compensating for reduced P/AI in cows without estrus.
