## Hechos clave (Qwen local)

- **Fact:** Yogurt is a cultured milk product traditionally prepared using Streptococcus thermophilus and Lactobacillus bulgaricus.
- **Fact:** Yogurt contains probiotics that offer important health benefits to the host, including enhanced intestinal barrier function, regulation of immune system, and positive influence on gut-brain axis.
- **Fact:** Adequate levels of live probiotics (10⁶ to 10⁹ cfu/mL) are essential for maintaining these benefits throughout the shelf life of the product.
- **Fact:** Cow milk is traditionally used in yogurt production, but there is a growing demand for non-cow milk products such as goat, sheep, camel, buffalo, and donkey milk.
- **Fact:** Goat milk has smaller fat globules, which may contribute to improved freezing stability compared to cow milk.
- **Fact:** Camel milk contains bioactive peptides and proteins with immune-enhancing and antibacterial properties.
- **Fact:** Donkey milk is potentially less allergenic than cow milk.
- **Fact:** Buffalo milk is richer in various nutrients compared to cow milk, although it has lower levels of vitamin E and cholesterol.
- **Fact:** Yak milk lipids exhibit higher lipolysis levels during digestion and release more unsaturated fatty acids (UFAs) than cow milk, making them a valuable alternative for infant nutrition.
- **Fact:** Plant-based yogurt (PBY) products are gaining popularity as lactose-free and vegan options, with almond, coconut, and oat milk being popular alternatives.
- **Fact:** The market for plant-based yogurt is projected to reach approximately $15.4 billion by 2033.
- **Fact:** Innovations in yogurt production include ultrasound-assisted fermentation and low-intensity pulsed electric fields to accelerate fermentation processes and enhance the texture and nutritional profile of yogurt.
- **Fact:** High-pressure processing ensures microbiological safety while preserving the sensory qualities of yogurt.
- **Technique:** The selection of different milk sources plays a crucial role in yogurt's microbial safety and quality control, with high-quality cow milk improving consistency and shelf life, and plant-based alternatives requiring specific fermentation strains and processes to achieve similar standards.
- **Technique:** Plant-based alternative sources include beans, grains, nuts, and fruits, which are increasingly recognized for their environmental and health benefits.
